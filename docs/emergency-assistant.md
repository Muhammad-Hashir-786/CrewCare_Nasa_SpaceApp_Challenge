# Emergency Assistant: Current Behavior and Safety Boundary

The persistent Emergency control opens a fast, mobile-responsive safety intake. Its first screen orders the spaceflight scenarios first: radiation exposure, decompression sickness, chest pain, collapse, severe breathing trouble, and sudden vision changes. Selecting a scenario immediately opens a local, numbered first-aid card and a live situation summary. The responder can mark steps complete, move step by step, have Nova read a step aloud, or use hands-free reading. The yes/no consciousness, breathing, and heavy-bleeding checks remain available without a long intake.

The scenario cards are simulated examples, not clinically or mission-approved care protocols. They avoid assigning a diagnosis, dose, exposure interpretation, or vehicle-specific action. Medicines are suggested only if the exact example item is confirmed in the physical kit and the safety checks are clear. The current sample inventory is illustrative and is not a claim about a real spacecraft kit. AI follow-up is optional; local first-aid steps do not depend on the model or network. The application does not deliver an alert or contact mission control.

## Not yet operational

- No clinically and mission-approved protocol library or vehicle-specific procedures have been integrated. The displayed cards require review by qualified flight-medicine and mission-safety authorities before operational use.
- No authenticated identity, medical officer directory, escalation integration, delivery receipt, or Mission Control emergency queue exists. The interface clearly says that human support is not connected.
- Emergency transcripts are not saved to a server. There is no emergency-session schema, authenticated audit trail, retention policy, or role-controlled review.
- Browser speech recognition and synthesis availability and processing depend on browser and device. Typed steps remain available if voice support is missing.
- The interface has not been tested in a browser/device matrix or validated for use under flight conditions.

## Required before operational emergency use

Obtain clinically and mission-approved procedures; define intended use and users; conduct hazard analysis, usability and software verification; implement identity/authorization, a monitored escalation channel with delivery acknowledgements, secure session persistence and audit; test network/provider failure and offline behavior; and complete the appropriate clinical/regulatory assessment.
